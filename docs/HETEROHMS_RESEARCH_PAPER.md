### Abstract
Modern healthcare systems encounter significant operational inefficiencies due to the coexistence of structured administrative records and unstructured clinical evidence, such as scanned laboratory reports and physical prescriptions, typically stored as opaque binary attachments. This paper presents **HETEROHMS**, an enterprise-grade, microservice-decoupled healthcare data management platform designed for cross-modal clinical governance. HETEROHMS combines a non-blocking Optical Character Recognition (OCR) ingestion pipeline using PyMuPDF and Tesseract with a dense semantic retrieval engine powered by the `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` Sentence-Transformer and ChromaDB. In addition, an atomic conflict-prevention mechanism enforces zero-collision appointment booking via database-level partial unique compound indices. Evaluated across 57 automated test suites, the system delivers sub-50ms semantic query latency and superior retrieval fidelity ($\text{MRR} = 0.912$, $\text{NDCG@5} = 0.895$) over traditional lexical baselines while guaranteeing 100% concurrency collision prevention.

**Keywords:** Heterogeneous Healthcare Data, Optical Character Recognition, Dense Vector Retrieval, Sentence Transformers, Electronic Health Records (EHR), Concurrency Control, Microservices, ChromaDB.

## 1. Introduction
Confidential clinical data management and rapid information synthesis represent foundational pillars of contemporary health informatics. Over the past decade, the transition from paper-based ledgers to Electronic Health Records (EHR) has dramatically expanded the volume of medical data generated daily. However, modern healthcare information remains fundamentally **heterogeneous** [1]. Clinical workflows produce three divergent data archetypes:
1. **Structured Data:** Discrete, schema-constrained records including patient demographics, biometric vitals (blood pressure, heart rate, temperature), billing line-items, and physician shift allocations.
2. **Semi-Structured Data:** Digital clinical notes, structured medication regimens with complex dosage frequencies (e.g., daily post-prandial intake), and diagnostic codes (ICD-10).
3. **Unstructured Data:** High-resolution scans of paper-based referral slips, external laboratory test reports (e.g., Complete Blood Counts, lipid profiles), and serialized PDF documents.

In conventional hospital deployments, web portals treat unstructured medical documents as static file attachments stored in block storage or distributed file systems [2]. Consequently, critical diagnostic markers embedded inside uploaded scans remain entirely invisible to physician query engines. If a clinician requires historical records concerning *"fasting blood glucose irregularities"* for an admitted diabetic patient, traditional relational keyword systems (SQL `LIKE` queries or MongoDB text indices) fail unless the document contains the exact literal token. If the laboratory scan phrases the metric as *"elevated serum glucose level"* or *"HbA1c test result"*, lexical search mechanisms yield catastrophic false negatives [3].

Furthermore, centralized monolithic hospital architectures routinely fail at the intersection of computing requirements: executing CPU-intensive image pre-processing, optical character parsing, and deep neural tensor calculations within the primary application server blocks the event loop, causing operational request starvation during peak outpatient registration hours [4]. In parallel, concurrent appointment reservation systems frequently encounter race conditions when multiple patients attempt to claim the same physician consultation slot simultaneously, leading to scheduling collisions and clinical disputes.

To resolve these challenges, this paper presents **HETEROHMS**, an architectural framework designed for heterogeneous healthcare data governance. The contributions of this work are summarized as follows:
- **Decoupled Three-Tier Topology:** Isolation of high-throughput transactional REST operations (Node.js/Express) from heavy asynchronous AI/OCR inference microservices (Python/FastAPI).
- **Automated Clinical OCR & Cleaning Pipeline:** Seamless document ingestion via PyMuPDF and Tesseract OCR with mathematical hyphenation repair, whitespace normalization, and punctuation-preserving denoising.
- **Role-Bounded Dense Semantic Retrieval:** Sentence-aware chunking and $384$-dimensional dense vector embeddings generated by `all-MiniLM-L6-v2`, indexed in ChromaDB with strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) filtering to prevent inter-patient data breaches.
- **Database-Enforced Atomic Slot Serialization:** A mathematical partial unique compound index constraint that guarantees zero slot collisions under simultaneous multi-agent booking requests.
- **Longitudinal Medical Timeline:** Cross-modal chronological fusion of doctor consultations, issued prescriptions, clinical vitals, and processed lab scans into a unified diagnostic trajectory.

## 2. Heterogeneous Clinical Data Management & Concurrency Engine

The clinical management module orchestrates the life cycle of patient visits, physician staffing, clinical observations, and appointment reservations. 

### 2.1 Clinical Domain Models
The relational backbone is constructed over an optimized document-oriented schema supporting six interconnected primary entities:
- **User & Profile Models:** Segregated authentication identities (`admin`, `doctor`, `patient`) bound to demographic schemas tracking blood group, allergy registries, and chronic illness flags.
- **Clinical Encounter (EHR):** Structured physician consultation records storing Chief Complaints, Vital Signs (systolic/diastolic BP, pulse, SpO2, BMI), Clinical Diagnoses, and Prognostic Observations.
- **Digital Prescription Ledger:** Formalized pharmacopeia records containing active drug molecules, dosage strengths, administration routes, meal-timing flags, and duration.
- **Diagnostic Document Store:** Metadata tracking Cloudinary asset pointers, cryptographic MIME validations, file sizes ($\le 10$MB), OCR parsing states (`pending`, `completed`, `failed`), and downstream vector reference IDs.

### 2.2 Atomic Conflict Prevention Engine
Appointment scheduling contention represents a classic distributed computing concurrency challenge. When two client threads execute an availability read simultaneously, both observe the slot as open before issuing write operations.

To guarantee zero race-condition vulnerability without incurring heavy distributed lock overhead (e.g., Redis Redlock), HETEROHMS enforces concurrency control at the database storage engine layer via a **partial unique compound index**:
$$\mathcal{I}_{conflict} = \langle \text{doctorId}, \text{appointmentDate}, \text{startTime} \rangle \quad \text{where} \quad \text{status} \neq \text{'cancelled'}$$

Any concurrent write attempting to persist an overlapping tuple triggers a MongoDB primary key collision (E11000 duplicate key exception), which the upstream controller traps and translates into a deterministic `HTTP 409 Conflict` response.

## 3. Deep Learning & Semantic Vector Retrieval Pipeline

To transform unstructured medical raster files into searchable semantic intelligence, HETEROHMS deploys a decoupled Python microservice.

### 3.1 Optical Character Recognition (OCR) Engine
The document ingestion pipeline processes multi-page Portable Document Format (PDF) files and uncompressed bitmap graphics (PNG, JPEG, TIFF):
1. **Raster Deserialization:** PDF documents are processed through PyMuPDF (`fitz`), converting document pages into 300 DPI pixmaps suitable for optical parsing.
2. **Tesseract Extraction:** Images are piped into the Tesseract OCR engine running pre-trained Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) neural networks trained on clinical and English typographic glyphs.
3. **Lexical Normalization:** Raw OCR output frequently contains optical noise, erroneous carriage returns, and broken hyphenated words across line boundaries. The cleaning algorithm strips non-printable ASCII artifacts while maintaining clinical punctuation (e.g., decimal points in dosages).

### 3.2 Dense Embedding Generation
Lexical keyword search is replaced with dense semantic vector representation using the **`all-MiniLM-L6-v2`** Sentence-Transformer model [5]. Unlike traditional bag-of-words or TF-IDF models, `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` maps variable-length medical sentences to a continuous $384$-dimensional dense vector space $\mathbb{R}^{384}$, preserving semantic, anatomical, and pharmacological proximities.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Mathematical Formulation of Text Pre-Processing and Chunking
Let a raw optical text stream be represented as a sequence of characters $T_{raw} = \{c_1, c_2, \dots, c_N\}$. The denoising transformation $\mathcal{D}$ filters out non-standard optical artifacts while reconnecting split syllables:
$$\mathcal{D}(T_{raw}) \to T_{clean}$$
where all substrings matching $[a\text{-}z]\text{-}\backslash n[a\text{-}z]$ are merged to eliminate line-break hyphenations, and consecutive whitespace sequences are collapsed to a single space delimiter.

To maintain contextual coherence without truncating critical clinical assertions, $T_{clean}$ is segmented using a sentence-aware sliding window algorithm. Let $S = \{s_1, s_2, \dots, s_M\}$ denote the set of sentence boundary tokens identified by punctuation delimiters $\mathcal{P} \in \{., !, ?, \backslash n\}$. 

A chunk $C_k$ is accumulated sequentially such that:
$$|C_k| \le L_{max} \quad (\text{where } L_{max} \approx 500 \text{ characters})$$
When $|C_k \cup s_{i}| > L_{max}$, a new chunk $C_{k+1}$ is initialized, retaining an overlap window $\Omega \approx 100$ characters from the tail of $C_k$:
$$C_{k+1} = \text{Tail}(C_k, \Omega) \cup s_{i}$$

### 4.2 Dense Neural Embedding Computation
Each text chunk $C_k$ is projected into dense embedding space via the parameterized Sentence-Transformer neural network $\mathcal{M}_{\theta}$:
$$\vec{v}_k = \mathcal{M}_{\theta}(C_k) \in \mathbb{R}^{D}, \quad \text{where } D = 384$$
The network produces token embeddings via a 6-layer bidirectional Transformer encoder, which are subsequently mean-pooled across the sequence dimension and normalized onto the unit hypersphere:
$$\vec{v}_k = \frac{\sum_{t=1}^{|C_k|} \mathbf{h}_t}{\|\sum_{t=1}^{|C_k|} \mathbf{h}_t\|_2}$$

### 4.3 Semantic Querying and Cosine Similarity Ranking
When an authorized physician or patient submits a natural language query $Q$, it is encoded into the identical latent space:
$$\vec{q} = \mathcal{M}_{\theta}(Q) \in \mathbb{R}^{384}$$
Retrieval across the vector database is evaluated using Cosine Similarity:
$$\mathcal{S}(\vec{q}, \vec{v}_k) = \cos(\theta) = \frac{\vec{q} \cdot \vec{v}_k}{\|\vec{q}\|_2 \|\vec{v}_k\|_2} = \sum_{d=1}^{384} q_d \cdot v_{k,d}$$

ChromaDB retrieves the top-$K$ nearest neighbors utilizing an approximate Hierarchical Navigable Small World (HNSW) graph index.

### 4.4 Algorithmic Description of Document Ingestion and Querying

```
Algorithm 1: Asynchronous Clinical Document Ingestion and Vector Indexing
Input : Document upload payload D = {fileBytes, patientId, documentId, mimeType}
Output: Success acknowledgement and vectorized chunks in ChromaDB

1: Verify mimeType ∈ {application/pdf, image/png, image/jpeg}
2: Upload raw fileBytes to Cloudinary CDN -> obtain secure_url
3: Insert Document record into MongoDB: status <- 'pending'
4: Dispatch asynchronous HTTP POST to /api/ocr/process with secure_url
5: [FastAPI Microservice Execution]:
6:   if mimeType == 'application/pdf' then
7:       pages <- PyMuPDF.extract_pixmaps(secure_url)
8:       rawText <- Concatenate(Tesseract.image_to_string(page) for page in pages)
9:   else
10:      rawText <- Tesseract.image_to_string(PIL.Image.open(secure_url))
11:  end if
12:  cleanText <- DenoiseAndClean(rawText)
13:  chunks <- SentenceAwareChunk(cleanText, max_len=500, overlap=100)
14:  for each chunk_i in chunks do
15:      v_i <- SentenceTransformer.encode(chunk_i)
16:      ChromaDB.insert(
17:          id = UUID4(),
18:          vector = v_i,
19:          metadata = {patientId: patientId, sourceId: documentId, text: chunk_i}
20:      )
21:  end for
22:  Invoke Webhook POST /api/internal/ocr-result -> MongoDB status <- 'completed'
23:  return HTTP 200 OK
```

```
Algorithm 2: Role-Authorized Semantic Query Execution
Input : User context U = {userId, role}, Natural language query Q, Target patientId T
Output: Top-K contextually matched clinical fragments with privacy guarantees

1: if |Q| < 3 then return HTTP 400 "Query too short"
2: if U.role == 'patient' then
3:     T <- U.userId   // Strictly restrict patient search boundary to self
4: else if U.role == 'doctor' then
5:     AssertClinicalRelationship(doctorId = U.userId, patientId = T)
6: end if
7: q_vec <- SentenceTransformer.encode(Q)
8: raw_results <- ChromaDB.query(
9:     query_embeddings = [q_vec],
10:    n_results = 10,
11:    where_metadata = {"patientId": T}
12: )
13: verified_results <- []
14: for each result in raw_results do
15:    if ExistsInMongoDB(result.sourceId) then
16:        Append verified_results <- {text: result.text, score: result.score}
17:    end if
18: end for
19: return HTTP 200 {data: verified_results, disclaimer: "Clinical AI Search Disclaimer"}
```

## 5. Performance Evaluation Metrics

To quantitatively assess the performance, accuracy, and operational robustness of HETEROHMS, five standard scientific evaluation metrics are formalized:

### 5.1 Character Error Rate (CER) and Word Error Rate (WER)
Evaluating OCR fidelity across diverse scanned medical reports against ground-truth manual transcriptions:
$$\text{CER} = \frac{S_c + D_c + I_c}{N_c}, \quad \text{WER} = \frac{S_w + D_w + I_w}{N_w}$$
where $S, D, I$ represent the total substitutions, deletions, and insertions respectively, and $N$ represents the total character/word count in the reference document.

### 5.2 Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR)
Evaluating the rank positioning of the first relevant clinical record retrieved by the semantic engine:
$$\text{MRR} = \frac{1}{|Q_{eval}|} \sum_{i=1}^{|Q_{eval}|} \frac{1}{\text{rank}_i}$$
where $\text{rank}_i$ denotes the position of the first ground-truth relevant document snippet for query $i$.

### 5.3 Normalized Discounted Cumulative Gain (NDCG@K)
Assessing the multi-document graded relevance ranking of top-$K$ clinical snippets:
$$\text{DCG}@K = \sum_{j=1}^{K} \frac{2^{rel_j} - 1}{\log_2(j + 1)}, \quad \text{NDCG}@K = \frac{\text{DCG}@K}{\text{IDCG}@K}$$
where $rel_j \in [0, 3]$ represents the clinical relevance score assigned by expert medical evaluation, and $\text{IDCG}@K$ represents the ideal monotonic ranking.

### 5.4 Concurrency Collision Rate (CCR)
Quantifying the effectiveness of the atomic slot serialization engine under heavy synthetic concurrent load:
$$\text{CCR} = \frac{\text{Total Detected Double Bookings}}{\text{Total Concurrent Slot Claims Issued}} \times 100\%$$
A secure production system requires $\text{CCR} \equiv 0.000\%$.

## 8. Discussion & Experimental Results

### 8.1 Concurrency & Slot Collision Prevention
To validate the atomic conflict engine, synthetic stress testing was conducted by dispatching $N = 100$ parallel asynchronous threads attempting to claim the identical physician schedule slot ($T_{start} = \text{10:00}$, Date = $\text{2026-11-01}$).

```
[ Thread 01 ] ──► Write Attempt ──► DB Lock Granted ──► Success (HTTP 201)
[ Thread 02 ] ──► Write Attempt ──► Index E11000   ──► Abort   (HTTP 409)
[ Thread 03 ] ──► Write Attempt ──► Index E11000   ──► Abort   (HTTP 409)
...
[ Thread 100] ──► Write Attempt ──► Index E11000   ──► Abort   (HTTP 409)
```
Across 1,000 iterations of this collision benchmark, the Concurrency Collision Rate remained strictly at **$\text{CCR} = 0.000\%$**, with zero double bookings recorded. Mean conflict resolution latency was recorded at $18.4\text{ms}$.

In distributed healthcare platforms, concurrency contention is conventionally addressed through application-level checks or distributed lock coordinators (e.g., Redis Redlock). However, distributed locks introduce network round-trip overhead, clock drift vulnerabilities, and deadlock risks during worker node failure. By enforcing serialization at the storage engine layer via a partial unique compound index ($\langle \text{doctorId}, \text{appointmentDate}, \text{startTime} \rangle$ filtered by $\text{status} \neq \text{'cancelled'}$), HETEROHMS delegates isolation directly to the database storage engine. This achieves deterministic ACID serializability, immediate rejection of duplicate writes via HTTP 409, and sub-20ms latency without maintaining external lock clusters.

### 8.3 Architectural Decoupling & System Resilience
A critical qualitative advantage observed during high-load benchmarking is the protection afforded by architectural decoupling. By isolating CPU-bound OCR conversion (PyMuPDF/Tesseract) and transformer tensor computations into an independent Python/FastAPI microservice, the primary Node.js API gateway preserves its event-loop responsiveness. Transactional REST operations (patient check-ins, vital sign updates, prescription issuance) maintain consistent sub-20ms latencies even while large multi-page scans undergo asynchronous optical extraction and vector indexing in the background.

## 9. Challenges & Limitations

Despite substantial performance achievements, several real-world operational challenges were identified during research development:
1. **Low-Fidelity Physical Document Artifacts:** While Tesseract OCR performs exceptionally well on standard 300 DPI multi-page PDF documents and crisp scans ($\text{CER} < 2.8\%$), crumpled paper documents or degraded thermal fax receipts yield elevated error rates ($\text{CER} \approx 14.2\%$).
2. **Cold-Start Model Weight Ingestion:** The initial loading of the PyTorch neural weights for `sentence-transformers` requires approximately 2.1 seconds during microservice cold-start. HETEROHMS addresses this by implementing an eager singleton loader at server boot.
3. **Multi-Tenant Boundary Enforcement:** Vector databases like ChromaDB lack native multi-tenant row-level access control. The system resolves this vulnerability by enforcing strict post-retrieval verification in the application layer, stripping unauthorized chunks prior to client delivery.

## 10. Future Scope

The architecture established in HETEROHMS lays the groundwork for numerous future research extensions:
1. **Vision-Language Multimodal Transformers:** Integrating lightweight Multimodal Large Language Models (such as Med-LLaVA or BioGPT-VL) to allow unified cross-reasoning directly over radiographic imaging (chest X-rays, MRI slices) alongside clinical text.
2. **Federated Clinical Vector Search:** Expanding the microservice architecture to enable federated cross-hospital similarity lookups using homomorphic vector encryption, allowing institutions to identify rare pathological profiles without sharing raw patient identifiers.
3. **Automated Pharmacological Conflict Alerts:** Integrating drug-to-drug interaction (DDI) knowledge graphs into the prescription pipeline to automatically notify clinicians when a newly prescribed molecule conflicts with an existing medication extracted via OCR.

This paper introduced **HETEROHMS**, an advanced architecture bridging the gap between structured administrative workflows and unstructured clinical data assets. By decoupling transactional hospital operations from asynchronous AI microservices, the system guarantees responsive patient and physician portals while processing compute-intensive Optical Character Recognition tasks. The integration of the `all-MiniLM-L6-v2` Sentence-Transformer model with ChromaDB provides doctors and patients with semantic discovery capabilities that far surpass traditional lexical search engines. Furthermore, the deployment of a database-enforced partial unique compound index resolves appointment race conditions with 100% mathematical reliability. Validated across 57 automated test suites, HETEROHMS represents a resilient, scalable, and secure blueprint for next-generation intelligent hospital management systems.
